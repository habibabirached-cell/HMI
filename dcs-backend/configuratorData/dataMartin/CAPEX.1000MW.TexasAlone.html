What Explore Data shows on Texas 1 GW. Each simulation’s number is Project LCOE at the study prices × (your blend ÷ the study blend). Charu writes one gas price for both turbines. The configurator has a gas price on each turbine, so the 7HA gas price scales 7HA fuel and the LM2500 gas price scales LM2500 fuel. When those two prices match, that is his single fuel factor. The bars split this simulation’s technology LCOE into CAPEX, fuel, and O&M so the algebra can be moved. The configurator reads those pieces from the workbook columns named below.

Scaling factors

solaradj = solar CAPEXuser1,200    windadj = wind CAPEXuser1,200    hdgtadj = 7HA CAPEXuser2,000

aeroadj = LM2500 CAPEXuser2,500    bessadj = battery CAPEXuser238    fueladj = gas priceuser3.50

Each technology’s LCOE

Solar LCOEuser = (Solar CAPEX LCOE0 × solaradj) + Solar OM LCOE

Wind LCOEuser = (Wind CAPEX LCOE0 × windadj) + Wind OM LCOE

BESS LCOEuser = (BESS CAPEX LCOE0 × bessadj) + BESS OM LCOE

7HA LCOEuser = (7HA CAPEX LCOE0 × hdgtadj) + (7HA Fuel LCOE0 × fueladj) + 7HA OM LCOE

LM2500 LCOEuser = (LM2500 CAPEX LCOE0 × aeroadj) + (LM2500 Fuel LCOE0 × fueladj) + LM2500 OM LCOE

Thermal LCOE

If 7HA units = 0, thermal LCOEuser = 0 The LM2500 does not enter the blend on a row with no 7HA.

If 7HA units = 3, thermal LCOEuser = 7HA LCOEuser The LM2500 LCOE is left out. The combined gas energy is priced as the 7HA.

Otherwise, thermal LCOEuser = (7HA LCOEuser × 7HA units × 384) + (LM2500 LCOEuser × LM2500 units × 32)(7HA units × 384) + (LM2500 units × 32) 384 MW is one 7HA. 32 MW is one LM2500. The same rule with every factor equal to 1 is the study thermal LCOE.
